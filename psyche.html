A room after Cupid & Psyche

Love · recognition · the light we share

The Lamp
Between Us

共照

What changes when the beloved can answer back?

Canova drew me in first: two faces held just apart, arms making a space around them. From there, this room follows Cupid and Psyche through touch, secrecy and recognition. The lamp becomes a question we bring to the art: can we learn to look together?

An interval held open

01 / Canova

Museum record

Antonio Canova
Cupid and Psyche, 1794
Plaster
The Metropolitan Museum of Art

View the collection record ↗

A reading

The arms make a circle, but the faces keep a little air between them. My eye stays in that interval. Love appears here as an approach that still leaves room for another person: close enough to feel, open enough to answer.

02 / Giovanni David

The wish to know

Museum record

Giovanni David
Curious Psyche, mid-1770s
Etching and aquatint; corrected proof with notations in pen and brown ink
The Metropolitan Museum of Art

View the collection record ↗

A reading

One small light redraws the room. Psyche leans toward a face that cannot yet look back. I feel both the urgency of wanting to know and the loneliness of having to discover someone in secret, with no voice answering the questions.

03 / François-Edouard Picot

Who holds the light?

Museum record

François-Edouard Picot
Cupid with Psyche Extinguishing the Lamp
Date not supplied by the museum
Pen and black ink with brown and gray wash and white heightening on brown prepared laid paper
National Gallery of Art, Washington
Ailsa Mellon Bruce Fund · 2000.60.2

View the collection record ↗

A reading

Here the winged figure reaches towards the lamp while the other rests. The light belongs to a decision made by one person. Set beside Curious Psyche, the drawing makes me ask who gets to choose what is visible, and when.

04 / Rodin

A body emerging

Museum record

Auguste Rodin
Cupid and Psyche, before 1893
Marble
The Metropolitan Museum of Art

View the collection record ↗

A reading

The rough stone occupies so much of this encounter. Against it, the polished bodies seem newly possible. I read the unfinished surface as the part of a relationship we cannot smooth away: what remains difficult, even while tenderness takes form.

05 / Pierre Reymond

A place at the table

Museum record

Pierre Reymond
The Wedding Feast of Cupid and Psyche, 1558
Painted enamel on copper, partly gilt
The Metropolitan Museum of Art

View the collection record ↗

A reading

After the close bodies, a whole table opens. Flowers fall; other faces gather. This small dish lets the story become social. I wonder what it means for a love once lived in secrecy to find a place among witnesses, with room for both lovers.

YOUSPEAK / words carried into the room

To see, and be answered.

These are contemporary reading lenses from YOUSPEAK. They help us ask something of the works; they do not claim to explain the artists’ intentions or the historical meaning of the myth.

darshanqing

Mutual beholding: recognition that happens between people, with each participating in how they become known.

Can I see you while leaving you free to correct what I see?

contrust

Trust generated through honest disagreement while remaining in relationship.

Can we speak about what frightened us, and keep listening?
