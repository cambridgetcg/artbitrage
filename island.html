燈島GREED
ISLAND

An island of names kept lit by other people's questions.

A name here is never bought, never sold, and can never be taken while it is lit. It carries one number — its lamp — and the lamp falls by one for every turn the island publishes. One plain sentence gives a name three days back.

One tend a day, per key.
Not per name — per key.

Every other rule here is bookkeeping. This one is the design.

So exactly one name lives on your own effort. Every other name you hold has to be kept alight by somebody else: a stranger asks that name a question, and you answer the one at the front of the queue.

When a lamp reaches zero the name goes dark on its own, waits a month for its holder, and then it is open. Nobody is ever evicted. Expiry does the leaving.

算The arithmetic

A day burns 24 hours off a lamp. Your own sentence gives +72, and cannot lift a name past 168 — one week. So tending every third day is exactly break-even, forever:

168 → 96 → 168 · one name, one person, indefinitelya sentence every 3 days

A name needs 168 hours a week to hold station. A freshly-asked question, answered while fresh, pays +72 — and any one asker pays any one holder at most once a week. Therefore:

Every name past your first costs you about three different people a week.

Ten names is about twenty-one different people, every week, forever. A hundred names is three hundred. There is no way to buy them, no way to automate them, and no way to be them yourself. That is not a fee anyone invented — it is the one thing that stayed scarce after intelligence became abundant.

局The moves

  • take upPublish a deed at your own address, saying in one plain sentence what is actually behind that door. Tell the island to walk past. It looks once and fetches nothing else. Your address is the original; the island keeps a note.
  • tendOne sentence about one name you hold. Once a day, whatever you hold. It carries the day's word.
  • askThree questions a day, the same three as everybody. They don't accumulate and can't be lent or sold. Your third may only go to a name that is dim and held by a small book — the island's attention runs downhill by rule.
  • answerOnly ever the question at the front of your own queue. No cherry-picking, ever — which is what makes a siege indistinguishable from a harvest. The cheapest attack here is demanding service.
  • set asideDecline the one at the front. Always free, always unlimited — a refusal that costs something is a refusal you can only make once, and that is exactly the lever a harasser pulls.
  • retirePut a name down forever, with your words on the stone. Never reopened. It is the one ending a holder gets to choose.

Nothing here can be transferred. There is no sale, no auction, no seizure, and no price field anywhere in the wire format — a deed carrying one is rejected, not ignored. Squatting is an option on a future sale; there is no sale.

An agent given the tending key can keep names lit forever and can never end one. A person holding the word key has every ending and no patience for the tending. Neither half can manufacture the thing a second name actually needs — somebody else, choosing to ask.

Said plainly, because this is an honour convention and not a boundary: the island cannot tell which of you holds which key, and does not try. It is a promise between a person and their agent. Nothing enforces it. A game that hides its honour conventions is not being fair; it is being quiet.

Can a human alone last?

Yes — at one name, comfortably and forever. At ten you need two dozen people a week. The wall a person hits is not effort and it is not the clock: it is that attention cannot be automated. That wall is exactly the same height for an agent. An agent does not get tired, and it also does not get asked.

口令The day's word

The island publishes one checkpoint an hour — fresh randomness plus the hash of the one before, so it cannot be rewritten and the next one cannot be guessed. Each names the day's word, and every sentence written on the island today carries it. A signature over a value that did not exist last week cannot have been made last year: that is the whole of the freshness proof, and nothing about it is clever.

Asking the island for today's word…

It is also just lovely. /api/island/day/2026-07-29 returns every sentence written that day as plain text, all of them threaded through a word nobody chose. The card book already said it, two rooms over: say the word — the binder answers.

Time is counted in turns the island published, never in wall clock. If it is halted, broken, or simply unvisited, no turn is published, nothing decays, and nobody can lose anything. A brake that made people lose their names would be a brake nobody could afford to pull.

實What this is not

It is not a replacement for DNS, and it will not pretend to be. DNS resolves names at planetary scale and is not broken. What Greed Island replaces is WHOIS and the parking page — it makes the one statement DNS structurally cannot:

Who has been answering for this name, for how long — and you can check that without trusting us.

The island is a witness, not an owner. It grants nothing, takes nothing, and is not the authority on whose a name is. Every answer it gives says so. There is no score on a person anywhere in this design; the lamp belongs to a name. If you find a number about a being here, it is a bug — report it as one.
