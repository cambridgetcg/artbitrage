the Slope

what is a wall, and what only sounds like one

We went looking for walls in a living garden — conditions without which a thing cannot work at all. Forty were proposed from real, fetched evidence. Thirty-nine fell, most of them to the very source that was meant to hold them up. The fortieth survives only as a definition. This is the record of that search, and it turns out to be more useful than the walls would have been.

↓ the count

Almost nothing here is a wall

This room began as a different machine. The plan was a checker: tell it what you have, and it tells you which layer of a garden ecosystem cannot stand yet, because each layer rests on the one below. That plan needed hard rules, so six researchers were sent to find them — and told that a rule only counts if the thing cannot work at all without the condition, never merely worse.

Forty came back. Then each was handed to an independent sceptic who fetched the same source again and judged it under that harsh standard. Thirty-nine were destroyed. What is striking is how: again and again the cited source refuted the rule in its own next breath. A government case study of turf failing over compacted subsoil turned out to record eighteen failures out of twenty-seven houses — a third of the same site did not fail, which is fatal to a floor. A soil-structure score defined by roots having given up says, in its own wording, "few roots, if any, and restricted to cracks" — roots, present, in cracks. A note on water refusing to cross a texture boundary explains on the same page that this is the founding principle of golf greens.

So the honest count is zero. And that changes the advice, in a direction worth hearing: you cannot fail at this by missing a rule, because there are almost no rules. There is no threshold to clear and no exam. There is only doing more or less of what helps, and the sizes of those effects are published below with their sources.

One place in this whole project did hold a real floor, and it is the place the work started: plants laid over sealed builder's rubble. That lives next door in 土 the Ground. Under the soil, walls exist. Above it, slopes.
